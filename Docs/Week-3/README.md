# Week 3 – Healthcare Workflows & Systems Thinking 

## Overview

Week 3 shifted the project from identifying the potential of artificial intelligence in emergency department triage to understanding how an AI system could realistically fit within an existing clinical workflow. This week's focus was mapping the patient journey through Mercer General Hospital's Emergency Department, identifying opportunities where Ai could provide meaningful clinical decision support and evaluating the operational constraints that influence implementation. The proposal was expanded to include a systems-thinking perspective through a workflow diagram, stakeholder analysis and workflow constraints. 

---

## Learning Objectives

Week 3 focused on understanding healthcare workflows and responsible AI deployment through:
- Identifying realistic AI intervention points within the existing Mercer General emergency department triage workflow
- Analysing workflow constraints that influence AI implementation
- Identifying key clinical stakeholders and their priorities
- Refining the research proposal using a systems-thinking approach

## Key Activities

- Analysed operational constraints affecting AI deployment within the emergency department
- Identified primary stakeholders involved in AI-assisted triage and documented their priorities
- Refined the preliminary proposal to incorporate workflow, systems-thinking and implementation considerations 

## Key Findings
The systems analysis demonstrated that successful implementation of an AI-assisted triage system depends as much on workflow integration as predictive performance. The proposed AI solution was designed to complement Mercer General's existing nurse-led triage process by supporting registration, triage decision-making, patient re-assessment, clinical investigations and discharge documentation while maintaining clinician oversight. Three major workflow constraints were identified, including fragmented paper-based documentation, investigation bottlenecks caused by shared diagnostic services, and limited physical capacity resulting from patient boarding. Stakeholder analysis further highlighted that successful deployment requires balancing the priorities of patients, clinicians, hospital administrators and IT teams to ensure the system improves patient safety and workflow efficiency without increasing clinical workload.
