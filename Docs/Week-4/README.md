# Week 4 – Ethics, Safety & Risk Awareness in Healthcare Technology 

## Overview

Week 4 focused on evaluating the ethical, safety and equity risks associated with developing and deploying an AI-assisted emergency department triage system. This involved examining how technical limitations, clinical workflows, human behaviour and unequal representation in healthcare could affect patient safety. A structured risk register was developed containing risks across Ai-technical, operational, ethical and equity categories. Each risk included its likelihood, impact, mitigation and a measurable signal of success. The three highest-priority risks: poor generalisation to Caribbean populations, automation bias and biased triage recommendations were examined further in a short risk memo. I also completed a root-cause analysis of a documented healthcare algorithm bias case and incorporated the findings into the preliminary proposal.

## Key Findings
The analysis found that the risks associated with AI-assisted triage may be reduced but cannot be completely eliminated. Technical accuracy alone would not be sufficient to justify deployment. The system would also need to demonstrate consistent performance across patient groups, compatibility with clinical workflows and the ability to operate safely during technical failures. AI should therefore function as a clinical decision-support tool rather than an autonomous replacement for triage nurses. Clinicians should retain responsibility for final triage decisions and be able to question or override AI recommendations. Broader validation across Caribbean hospitals and longer-term clinical evaluation would be required before considering nationwide deployment. Overall, Week 4 established an ethics and safety framework for the project by identifying foreseeable harms, proposing measurable safeguards and recognising the remaining uncertainty associated with deploying AI in a high-stakes clinical environment.
